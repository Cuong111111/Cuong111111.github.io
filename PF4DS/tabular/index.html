DUPLICATES

Deduplication changes the dataset materially

Removing 31,994 exact duplicate rows reduces the data from 119,390 to 87,396 records. The observed cancellation proportion shifts from 37.04% to 27.49%, so results must state clearly which version of the data they describe.

Missing-value rates by feature

Company is missing in about 94% of rows and agent in about 14%; country is missing much less often, and only four children values are absent. Company should not be blindly imputed as an ordinary feature; decide whether its sparse coverage warrants dropping, a missing category, or another documented treatment.

IQR SCREENING

Outlier flags are a review aid, not a delete rule

The notebook flags 3,005 lead-time values (2.52%) and 3,793 ADR values (3.18%) with the IQR rule. ADR reaches -6.38 and 5,400 in the raw data, so extreme or implausible values deserve validation; the rule alone cannot distinguish errors from unusual bookings.

Distributions of selected numerical features

Lead time and ADR have long right tails, while guest counts and night counts are concentrated at small values. Children and babies are zero for most bookings. Transformations or robust scaling may help some models, but should be chosen after checking model behavior.

Lead-time boxplot

The central range is much shorter than the longest lead times, producing a visible upper tail and IQR flags. Values as high as 737 days should be checked for validity, but long advance booking windows can be genuine.

Average daily rate (ADR) boxplot

Most ADR values sit in a relatively narrow range, with a long upper tail and an extreme maximum of 5,400. The raw minimum is negative (-6.38); inspect such records and document any correction or exclusion before modeling.

OTHER GROUPS

Rates vary across customer and market segments

Transient customers have a 40.75% cancellation rate (89,613 bookings), versus 10.23% for Group customers (577 bookings). Groups market segment is 61.06% (19,811 bookings), while Direct is 15.34% (12,606 bookings). Group sizes differ, so rates should be read with counts.

Cancellation rate by deposit type

Non Refund bookings show a 99.36% cancellation rate in the saved notebook summary, compared with 28.38% for No Deposit and 22.22% for Refundable. The Refundable group contains only 162 bookings; investigate booking definitions and sample size before drawing broad conclusions.

Cancellation rate by arrival month

Monthly rates vary: the chart peaks around June and is lowest around November. This suggests seasonality in the observed bookings, but the plot combines years; it does not separate seasonal effects from changes between years or booking mix.

03 / DATA PREPARATION & ASSESSMENT

Prepare carefully before modeling.

The supplied notebook covers EDA and feature preparation; it does not report fitted-model results.

01

Handle missing values deliberately

Company is absent for 94.31% of rows and agent for 13.69%. The notebook imports imputation tools, but final imputation decisions should be fitted on training data only. Evaluate whether company adds useful signal given its sparse coverage.

02

Guard against target leakage

Reservation status and reservation status date describe the outcome and are excluded from the predictor set in the notebook. Keep that exclusion; otherwise the model could learn information unavailable at the time a booking needs a prediction.

03

Check the deduplication effect

Exact duplicate removal drops 31,994 rows and changes the cancellation share from 37.04% to 27.49%. Confirm whether repeated rows are true duplicates or meaningful repeated booking records, and report results against a clearly defined dataset version.

04

No model metrics are available yet

Although model classes and metric functions are imported, the notebook ends after defining the cleaned feature matrix and target. Next, use a stratified holdout split, train a baseline and candidate models, then report precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC, and a confusion matrix for cancellation.
